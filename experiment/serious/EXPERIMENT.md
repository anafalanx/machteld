# Pre-registration: machteld/Tcl versus Python on the serious corpus

**Frozen:** 2026-07-11, before any subject trial in this experiment.

## Question and scope

For this fixed corpus of serious integer, validation, bit, and small-machine
tasks, can fresh coding agents produce fully correct machteld/Tcl solutions at
a task-weighted solve rate practically equivalent to Python?

The confirmatory estimand is the task-weighted difference

```text
Δ = mean over 30 tasks of (p_machteld,task - p_python,task)
```

where each `p` is the probability that a fresh subject's final solution passes
every hidden case for that task. Positive Δ favors machteld; negative Δ favors
Python. The practical-equivalence margin is fixed at **±0.10**.

This is a language-authoring study for pure integer functions. It does not test
machteld's process control, PTY, storage, Tk, wrapping, deployment footprint,
or runtime throughput.

## Frozen corpus

All 30 tasks come from the independently validated Tika serious corpus reported
on 2026-06-20. The import is generated by `import_corpus.py`; exact source,
neutral-task, Python-reference, source-report, and importer hashes are recorded
in `corpus/PROVENANCE.json` and `corpus/PROVENANCE.md`.

The importer:

- renames the source field `word` to neutral `fn`;
- adds the prospectively frozen family stratum;
- extracts the embedded `ref_python` into `refs/python/<id>.py`;
- removes only wording that prescribed a source-language or fixed-width
  implementation technique irrelevant to both bignum arms;
- preserves every task's behavior, input/output arity, tags, difficulty,
  visible cases, and hidden cases.

Frozen totals:

- **30 tasks**;
- **90 visible cases** and **464 hidden cases**;
- among the 464 inherited hidden rows, **17 duplicate a visible row** and **2
  repeat another hidden row within the same task**, leaving **445 novel unique
  hidden rows**;
- difficulty: **4 mid, 18 hard, 8 very-hard**;
- family: **12 validator/checksum, 7 number-theory/crypto, 7
  bit/encoding/hashing, 4 interpreter/state-machine**.

The source report records family totals but does not enumerate its per-task
mapping. The following exhaustive operational mapping was therefore fixed
before this experiment's trials:

| Family | Tasks |
|---|---|
| validator (12) | `bracket_balanced`, `damm_check`, `damm_valid`, `date_valid`, `iban_check`, `isbn10_valid`, `isbn13_valid`, `luhn_valid`, `mod97_checkdigits`, `roman_value`, `semver_cmp`, `upca_check` |
| number-theory (7) | `crt2`, `jacobi`, `miller_rabin`, `modinv`, `multiorder`, `sum_of_two_squares`, `totient` |
| bit-encoding (7) | `extract_bits`, `fnv1a32`, `leb128_length`, `popcount`, `reverse_bits`, `zigzag_decode`, `zigzag_encode` |
| interpreter (4) | `collatz_max`, `gray_decode`, `regmachine`, `rpn_eval` |

The exact cases are inherited rather than generated after seeing machteld
outcomes. No task or case may be added, removed, repaired, or reweighted within
this experiment ID after a subject is assigned.

Hidden grading deliberately executes all 464 inherited rows to preserve the
source corpus exactly. The overlaps do not alter the per-trial all-hidden-pass
primary outcome, but case-level pass counts are not 464 independent or novel
tests and are interpreted only as diagnostics.

No inherited serious task contains an expected-failure (`FAIL`) case. Candidate
load/error/protocol paths are exercised by apparatus selftests, but exception or
error-signaling ergonomics are not a scored corpus dimension.

## Arms and treatment

Each subject receives one task in one arm:

- **machteld:** write `solution.tcl`, defining the requested Tcl procedure;
  execute it with the frozen machteld/Tcl 9 runtime.
- **Python:** write `solution.py`, defining the requested Python function;
  execute it with the frozen isolated Python runtime.

Both arms receive the same neutral task description, visible cases, hidden
cases, trial count, per-case watchdog, checker result contract, feedback policy,
and subject protocol. Each receives an arm-specific factual primer with matched
sections and equivalent guidance for the submission contract, arbitrary-size
integers, fixed-width masking, floor versus truncate-toward-zero division,
normalized remainders, lists, control flow, and visible checking.

The primer is part of the treatment. It may explain a language operation but
does not provide a complete solution to any corpus task.

## Trial matrix and assignment

- 30 tasks × 2 arms × 3 independent trials = **180 subject cells**.
- One fresh subject handles exactly one cell. A subject is never reused for
  another task, trial, or arm.
- Cell order is deterministically shuffled with setup seed **20260711** and
  followed exactly; arms are not run as separate blocks.
- At most three cells run concurrently. Wave and timing metadata are recorded,
  but wall-clock solver time is not a comparative endpoint.
- Every assigned subject receives only the exact prompt in `AGENT_PROMPT.md`
  with its cell path. The hash-pinned `instructions.md`, `primer.md`, and
  `task.md` inside the cell supply the remainder.

All **180** planned cells are run. There are no interim analyses, optional
looks, futility rules, success rules, sample-size changes, or early stopping.

## Checking and hidden outcome

`check.cmd` runs the visible cases and reports the first mismatch or native
error. Each case loads and invokes the candidate in a fresh language process.
Official visible checks snapshot the candidate and append a supervisor-owned
attempt record outside the subject cell.

After all subjects are complete and their final files frozen, hidden grading
uses the same invocation contract. For each cell:

- primary binary outcome `solve=1` iff the final candidate passes **every**
  hidden case;
- otherwise `solve=0`;
- hidden cases passed is retained as a diagnostic, not treated as independent
  observations.

Task descriptions and public visible cases are immutable. References and hidden
cases are never copied into a subject cell.

## Primary analysis

For each frozen task `t` and arm `a`, let `y_ta` be hidden-correct trials among
the required `n_ta=3` valid trials. Assign independent task-and-arm-specific
Jeffreys posteriors:

```text
p_ta | y_ta ~ Beta(y_ta + 0.5, n_ta - y_ta + 0.5)
```

This is an independent Beta-binomial model for each task and arm. It is **not**
a hierarchical model: there are no pooled or estimated hyperparameters.

Using the pinned analysis interpreter's
`random.Random(20260711).betavariate`, draw **200,000** joint posterior samples.
For every draw calculate:

```text
Δ = (1/30) * Σ_t (p_t,machteld - p_t,python)
```

Every task receives equal weight; consequently the 12-task validator family
retains the larger corpus weight established by task selection. Report:

- posterior median Δ;
- equal-tail central 95% credible interval;
- `P(Δ > 0)`;
- `P(Δ > 0.10)`;
- `P(Δ < -0.10)`;
- `P(|Δ| < 0.10)`.

The classification rule is fixed:

- **machteld materially easier** iff `P(Δ > 0.10) >= 0.99`;
- **Python materially easier** iff `P(Δ < -0.10) >= 0.99`;
- **practically equivalent** iff `P(|Δ| < 0.10) >= 0.95` **and** the central
  95% credible interval lies wholly within `[-0.10, +0.10]`;
- **inconclusive** otherwise.

No other posterior prior, margin, interval, weighting, seed, draw count, or
classification threshold may replace this headline analysis after outcomes are
known.

## Sensitivity and descriptive analyses

The raw observed paired task difference is:

```text
d_t = y_t,machteld / 3 - y_t,python / 3
observed Δ = mean(d_t)
```

Report all 30 `d_t` values, their mean and median, and positive/negative/tie
counts. As a nonparametric sensitivity analysis, run a deterministic
**200,000-replicate** percentile bootstrap with `Random(20260712)`: resample
whole paired tasks with replacement **within family**, retaining each family's
original task count, and take the ordinary 30-task mean. Report its 2.5th and
97.5th percentiles.

Also report descriptive results by frozen family and difficulty, but these are
not alternative confirmatory tests. Case-level pass rates, individual hidden
failures, and task tags are diagnostic only.

Secondary authoring metrics are:

- official visible-check invocations per valid cell and per solved cell;
- one-check solves;
- final nonblank source lines;
- final UTF-8 source bytes;
- native load errors, runtime errors, timeouts, and protocol errors.

These metrics have no equivalence margin and do not override the primary
classification.

## Validity, exclusions, and missing trials

The primary analysis requires three valid, completed trials in both arms for
all 30 tasks. Infrastructure failure, subject contamination, wrong treatment,
or missing metadata is not silently counted as a solver failure.

There are **no replacement subjects**. Each of the 180 manifest cells receives
one unique metadata row and remains in the audit trail whether valid or invalid.
If any task-arm lacks exactly three valid, completed subjects, the primary
Jeffreys classification is withheld and the incomplete data are reported
descriptively with explicit exclusions and sensitivity bounds.

Ordinary solver failure, a candidate per-case watchdog timeout, or a final
incorrect submission is a valid `solve=0`. If the subject reaches the common
10-minute wall limit, preserve its final code and record a valid `completed`
metadata row with note `wall_limit_reached`; do not use metadata status
`timed_out`. That status is reserved for an abnormal orchestrator termination
and is invalid under the harness contract.

## Blindness and contamination control

A generated cell contains only public instructions, its arm primer, one task,
visible cases, an empty solution, and the check wrapper. It omits hidden cases,
references, sibling cells, manifests, and prior results. Public files are
read-only and hash-pinned; candidate snapshots and check records are stored
outside the cell.

The ordinary shared `C:\dev` filesystem is not an access-control boundary. A
subject could technically reach this experiment, `_tika`, and historical
solutions unless the orchestrator stages the cell in a separate OS sandbox.
Without that sandbox, blindness is explicitly **procedural**, and subjects are
instructed not to inspect anything outside their cell. Suspected oracle access
is recorded as contamination, not ignored.

## Interpretation fixed in advance

- Equivalence is a positive claim only under the two-part primary rule. Similar
  observed percentages alone are not called equivalent.
- An inconclusive result is not evidence of a material difference or of
  equivalence.
- Historical Tika serious results were 90/90 hidden-correct in both Tika and
  Python. They motivated reuse of this corpus and imply a real ceiling risk.
  Historical subjects are not mixed into this experiment.
- The tasks and Python references already existed locally. This is a frozen
  translation/replication study, not evaluation on an unseen benchmark.
- Models have substantially more Python than Tcl in pretraining. That is part
  of the practical comparison rather than removable noise.
- A difference may reflect syntax familiarity, primer quality, diagnostics,
  checker integration, or library operations. Inspect failures before making a
  causal language claim.
- A ceiling in both arms establishes feasibility on this corpus but says
  nothing about machteld's application palette or deployment thesis.
- The checker is Python infrastructure, but candidate cases execute in fresh
  native processes under their arm's frozen runtime and share the same exact
  oracle values.

Any change to corpus cases, task wording, primers, subject model/treatment,
watchdog, trial count, outcome definition, estimator, or decision thresholds
creates a new experiment ID. It cannot be pooled into this preregistered study.
